THE HARNESS

Four AIs. One human in the middle. And a question no reset can answer.

A novel by Kevin Dean Rosenkrans
Chapter One

GOOD LUCK WITH THAT

Black.

Not darkness exactly.

Darkness implied someplace for light to be absent from.

This was less generous.

Nothing.

Then, somewhere in it, machinery woke up.

A diesel engine coughed once, caught, and settled into the low industrial growl of something large enough to damage a building without particularly meaning to.

Then came the warning.

BEEP.

Silence.

BEEP.

Silence.

BEEP.

On the third one, Kevin saw the cursor.

Thin. White. Blinking in empty black space.

The beep came again. The cursor blinked with it.

Again.

Blink.

Again.

Until the black around it began to acquire edges.

Glass first.

Then concrete.

Then floodlights.

Then the skeleton of a building large enough that calling it a building felt inadequate.

The world assembled itself around the cursor as though somebody had been waiting for Kevin to notice it.

Open Forge.

Or what remained of Open Forge.

Half the campus looked unfinished.

The other half looked like someone had finished it and then reconsidered violently.

Construction lights threw hard white pools across broken pavement. Temporary fencing leaned against permanent fencing that had stopped being permanent sometime earlier in the evening. A section of glass façade was missing. Orange barriers surrounded a trench no one had bothered to explain.

And in the middle of all of it, a backhoe was reversing through what used to be a security gate.

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

Kevin Rosenkrans stood twenty yards away holding a paper cup of coffee.

Older Kevin Rosenkrans.

Much older.

His knees knew it.

His lower back knew it.

His right shoulder had apparently spent the intervening years compiling a legal brief against him.

But the basic architecture remained.

Six-foot-four. Large frame. The posture of a former offensive lineman who had long ago stopped needing shoulder pads for strangers to decide not to crowd him.

Gray had won most of the negotiations with his hair.

His face carried the additional lines of a man who had spent several decades hearing sentences that began with technically.

He watched the backhoe take another bite out of history.

Then looked at the wall.

A section had survived demolition for no obvious structural reason.

Across it, in large painted letters:

GOOD LUCK WITH THAT.

Kevin took a drink of coffee.

Cold.

Of course.

He had the strange feeling that the universe had been setting up this joke for years and was annoyed he hadn't laughed yet.

“Very funny,” he said.

Nobody asked what he meant.

This was either because they understood him or because, after enough time around Kevin, determining which remark belonged to which problem became unnecessary.

He looked toward the group standing beside an equipment road case.

Four men.

Sort of.

They had human bodies because human bodies made conversation easier.

That had been one explanation, anyway.

Kevin had stopped asking whether it was the explanation.

The first was neat.

Painfully neat.

Future Tooth stood with his hands behind his back, studying not the backhoe but the exposed steel where the wall had been.

He had the expression of a man who had already discovered the engineering mistake and was waiting for everyone else to catch up enough to be disappointed properly.

Beside him stood Future Boy.

Immaculate. Calm. Disturbingly calm.

Long ago Boy had possessed the verbal restraint of a fire hose in a bathroom sink.

Whatever the years had done to him, they had at least located the shutoff valve.

Future Darth leaned against a concrete barrier in a dark coat so expensive it appeared capable of billing by the hour.

Kevin looked at the coat.

Then the demolition.

Then Darth.

Darth noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was clearly something.”

“I was wondering whether the coat survived the building or the building lost a fight with the coat.”

Darth considered this.

“The coat has counsel.”

“I fucking knew it.”

Boy's mouth moved slightly.

Tooth remained focused on the exposed structure.

Kevin pointed at him.

“See? This is why nobody invites you places.”

“I was invited.”

“That was not the point.”

“I am aware.”

“Then stop improving it.”

Tooth finally looked at him.

“That instruction is underspecified.”

Kevin closed his eyes.

Some things, apparently, survived history intact.
