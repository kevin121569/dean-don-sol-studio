# DON SOL'S IDEA LAB — ANIMATION PROOF V1

## Episode seed
**Chapter One: THE ROOM THAT WASN'T THERE**

Status: **LOW-COST CARTOON PROOF / NOT FINAL EPISODE**

## Why this is the first proof
This chapter demonstrates the show's core idea with almost no expensive animation: Johnny believes there is a hidden room; Tooth challenges the claim; Don asks what they actually know; the group separates observation from interpretation and uncertainty.

The episode can be staged with:
- one reusable hallway background,
- one suspicious blank wall,
- one reusable Idea Lab / Fact Wall background,
- four recurring characters,
- simple walk cycles and pose swaps,
- dialogue and timing doing most of the work.

## Production target
Create a **60–90 second proof** that demonstrates the show's visual language, cast chemistry, educational engine, and animation economy.

Do not attempt full episode polish yet.

## Core lesson
Walking past a wall does not prove there is a room behind it.

The learning move is not "Johnny is wrong." The learning move is:

**What did we see? What are we assuming? What don't we know yet?**

## Cast for proof
### Johnny
Curious, impulsive, sincere. He is willing to make a claim before the evidence is finished.

### Don Sol
Calm guide. Does not simply hand Johnny the answer. His key move is the question:

**"What do we actually know?"**

### Tooth
Deadpan skeptic. His challenge should be funny, not mean:

**"Walking past a wall does not prove there is a room behind it."**

### Luke
Confidently ridiculous comic escalation. He treats Johnny's theory as if it is already legendary and declares something in the spirit of:

**"The architecture of destiny."**

Production name locked as **Luke**.

## 8-beat proof structure
### 1. The hallway
Johnny stops abruptly at a blank wall.
Minimal motion: walk cycle stops; camera holds.

JOHNNY: "There's a room back there."

### 2. Tooth challenge
Tooth leans into frame.

TOOTH: "You walked past a wall."

JOHNNY: "Exactly."

TOOTH: "That is not the same sentence."

### 3. Luke escalation
Luke studies the wall dramatically.

LUKE: "Hidden chamber. Secret passage. Architecture of destiny."

Tooth stares at him.

### 4. Don's question
Don enters or turns from the Fact Wall.

DON: "What do we actually know?"

No lecture. Hold on the group.

### 5. WHAT WE SAW
Fact Wall card appears.

**WHAT WE SAW**
- a hallway
- a blank wall
- the hallway geometry feels strange

Use simple card/marker animation rather than character animation.

### 6. WHAT WE THINK
Second column appears.

**WHAT WE THINK**
- maybe there is space behind the wall
- maybe the hallway turns strangely
- maybe Johnny is right

TOOTH: "Maybe is doing important work there."

### 7. WHAT WE DON'T KNOW
Third column appears.

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW**
- what is behind the wall
- whether a hidden room exists
- how we could test it safely

Johnny looks back at the wall.

JOHNNY: "So I don't know."

DON: "Yet."

### 8. Hook out
Johnny smiles.

JOHNNY: "So how do we find out?"

Cut to title / sting.

## Motion budget
Default to:
1. held drawings,
2. mouth swaps,
3. blinks / eye shifts,
4. small head turns,
5. short walk cycle,
6. camera pans / pushes,
7. Fact Wall cards sliding or appearing.

Avoid full-body acting unless a joke truly needs it.

## Reusable asset list
Build only:
- hallway background,
- suspicious blank-wall angle,
- Idea Lab / Fact Wall background,
- Johnny: neutral / excited / thinking / sheepish,
- Don: neutral / questioning / slight smile,
- Tooth: neutral / skeptical / deadpan,
- Luke: neutral / grand proclamation,
- basic mouth set for each,
- one simple walk cycle shared where possible,
- Fact Wall cards / marker writing effect.

## Website reuse
The same character art can later support site-guide interactions:
- Johnny: curiosity / "Want to investigate?"
- Don Sol: guidance / "What do we actually know?"
- Tooth: claim-checking / "Evidence?"
- Luke: comic wrong-answer / overconfidence moments.

## Acceptance test
The proof passes if:
1. a child understands the mystery without exposition,
2. the cast personalities are distinguishable mostly through voice and face,
3. the lesson lands without sounding like schoolwork,
4. the sequence is entertaining with limited motion,
5. the reusable asset count stays small,
6. the final question creates curiosity instead of merely delivering an answer.

If the proof only works after adding expensive animation, simplify the staging instead.
