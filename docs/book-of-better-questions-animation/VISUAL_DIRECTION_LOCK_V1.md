# THE BOOK OF BETTER QUESTIONS — VISUAL DIRECTION LOCK V1

Status: **APPROVED WORKING PRODUCTION TARGET**

## Purpose
Lock the user-approved visual direction for the animated adaptation so future production work does not drift into expensive or inconsistent redesign.

## Core visual rule
**Character and story first. Animation complexity second.**

The world should feel warm, intelligent, playful, and visually alive without requiring feature-animation budgets.

## Approved main cast look
### Johnny
- Young human lead.
- Brown hair, red hoodie, jeans, sneakers.
- Curious and expressive.
- Reads immediately as the point-of-view kid.
- Strong face acting is more important than complex body mechanics.

### Don Sol
- Warm older mentor figure.
- Silver/white hair and beard.
- Brown/tan long coat over dark clothes.
- Calm, wise, amused rather than preachy.
- Should feel like the person who asks the question that changes the room.

### Tooth
- Small friendly AI robot.
- White shell, dark face screen, bright blue expressive eyes/mouth.
- Question-mark emblem.
- Curious, fast-thinking, skeptical, funny.
- Simple limb design for cheap repeatable animation.

### Luke
- Black dog with red neckwear/scarf accent.
- Expressive ears, eyes, tail, and posture.
- Loyal, playful, theatrically overconfident.
- Humor should come from behavior and timing, not constant dialogue.
- Production name locked as **Luke**.

## Approved world look
### The Idea Lab
A cozy workshop / study / invention space with:
- warm wood,
- lamps,
- shelves,
- tools,
- paper notes,
- mystery-board energy,
- selective blue technology light.

It should feel like a place where questions are physically worked on.

### Reusable core locations
1. Main Idea Lab room.
2. Fact Wall.
3. Round Table.
4. Build Bench.
5. Magic Keyboard station.
6. Hallway / ordinary-world investigation spaces.

## Fact Wall visual language
The Fact Wall uses three instantly readable zones:
- **WHAT WE SAW**
- **WHAT WE THINK**
- **WHAT WE DON'T KNOW**

These should function as recurring visual grammar, not as a classroom worksheet. Cards, notes, drawings, evidence, and jokes can populate the zones.

## Animation doctrine
Use limited animation deliberately:
- held poses,
- blink and eye changes,
- mouth swaps,
- head turns,
- hand gestures,
- short walk cycles,
- tail/ear motion for Luke,
- small robot-arm motions for Tooth,
- camera pans and push-ins,
- foreground/background parallax,
- card slides and marker-writing effects,
- occasional larger motion when the story earns it.

Do not attempt constant full-body animation.

## Emotional rule
The audience should believe the characters are alive because of:
1. voice,
2. timing,
3. facial acting,
4. relationships,
5. writing,
6. sound,
7. selective motion.

Not because every frame is redrawn.

## Production economy
Build assets to be reused across episodes:
- neutral / happy / worried / thinking / surprised / deadpan / excited expressions,
- a small mouth set per speaking character,
- front / 3-quarter / profile where useful,
- basic standing and sitting poses,
- one reusable walk cycle where appropriate,
- modular hands/arms for pointing and holding props,
- reusable location backgrounds.

## Upgrade path
Future budget may increase:
- motion smoothness,
- number of poses,
- background detail,
- camera movement,
- lighting,
- effects,
- lip sync,
- shot complexity.

But future upgrades must preserve the approved character identities, relationships, reasoning engine, and story-first philosophy.

**We are allowed to upgrade the world later. We are not allowed to postpone telling the stories now.**
