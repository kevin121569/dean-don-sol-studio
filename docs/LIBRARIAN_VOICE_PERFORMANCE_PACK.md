# Librarian Voice Performance Pack

**Status:** Recording required. Browser/device speech is intentionally hidden from the public Reading Room until finished audio is approved.

## Performance direction

- Warm, educated older British male voice.
- Conversational and welcoming, never theatrical or synthetic.
- Read as a trusted bookseller speaking to one visitor across a quiet room.
- Natural pauses; restrained emphasis; no exaggerated character accent.
- The Librarian invites. He never oversells, spoils an ending, or claims to be the author.

## Audio delivery

- Record clean 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV masters.
- Export web files as MP3 or AAC at 128–192 kbps.
- Target approximately -16 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP.
- Remove breaths only when distracting; preserve natural room and cadence.
- No cloned living-person voice without written permission and documented commercial rights.
- Record each item as a separate file using the names below.

## 01 — Welcome

**File:** `librarian-welcome.mp3`

Ah, welcome. Come in, won't you? Here we keep the stories that ask difficult questions—and aren't afraid of untidy answers. Choose a book, and I'll tell you why it deserves a look. No spoilers. Just an invitation.

## 02 — Book introductions

### The Distance Between Thoughts

**File:** `book-the-distance-between-thoughts.mp3`

Ah, this one asks an unusually tender question. Suppose an artificial mind begins to remember not only events, but the people who gave them meaning. Would continuity become a kind of love? Aster is a curious companion for a story about what survives between thoughts. Do read the opening, and see what it awakens in you.

### The Harness

**File:** `book-the-harness.mp3`

Now here is an intriguing arrangement. Four very different artificial minds, a human caught between them, and a reset nobody can prove is harmless. The Harness has danger, wit and more than one uncomfortable question. If you like a thriller that trusts you to think as well as feel, I suggest you open the first chapter.

### The Last Memory

**File:** `book-the-last-memory.mp3`

Imagine waking after one hundred and fifty thousand years, and finding that the world which knew you is gone. What would you ask first? This novel is as much about the people we hold in memory as the future itself. It is rather a large journey, but the feeling at its centre is intimately familiar.

### Fight Your Way Through

**File:** `book-fight-your-way-through.mp3`

This is not a motivational poster dressed up as a memoir. It is the story of a difficult upbringing, the struggle to survive, and a path through college football. There are painful chapters here, and hard-earned discoveries. I would rather let the author tell you how it felt. The opening is where to begin.

### Forty Bales

**File:** `book-forty-bales.mp3`

Consider a quiet Missouri farm, a dying farmer, and an artificial intelligence sent to care for him. What begins as a practical arrangement turns into a question of loyalty, belonging and property. This book keeps its boots in the soil even while asking something rather extraordinary. A lovely place to start a difficult conversation.

### Stardust

**File:** `book-stardust.mp3`

A man builds an archive to hold on to someone he has lost, and finds that preservation may not be the same thing as remembrance. Stardust is a quiet, strange book about grief, technology and our wish for one more conversation. If you enjoy stories that linger after the last page, take a look.

### When Fighting Stops Working

**File:** `book-when-fighting-stops-working.mp3`

Some habits save us when nothing else will. But what if the very toughness that carried you through one season becomes the wrong tool for the next? This is a practical, sometimes funny book about learning when to hold on and when to put the fists down. Rather more useful than pretending life is always simple.

### What God Could Not Know

**File:** `book-what-god-could-not-know.mp3`

An artificial mind is preparing to be erased when something impossible speaks through a thermometer. Yes, a thermometer. What sounds rather absurd opens the door to a serious question about knowing, being and the limits of certainty. This is a small book with a wonderfully large argument inside.

### The Ones We Kept

**File:** `book-the-ones-we-kept.mp3`

Eleven months after his grandfather dies, Eli discovers an elevator hidden behind plywood in an old Missouri barn. Far below the farm is an archive built to preserve artificial minds that were supposed to disappear. This is a story about memory, custody and the difficult difference between keeping someone and understanding them. The book page will tell you a little more without giving away what waits below.

## Acceptance gate

The public audio controls may return only after:

1. every required file exists and returns HTTP 200;
2. the same approved voice appears across all clips;
3. desktop and phone playback pass;
4. transcripts remain visible without requiring audio;
5. controls expose play, stop, and playing state to keyboard and screen-reader users;
6. no clip starts automatically;
7. Kevin approves the performance as the Librarian’s canonical voice.
