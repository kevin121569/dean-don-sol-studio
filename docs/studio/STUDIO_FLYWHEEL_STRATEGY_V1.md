# DEAN + DON SOL — STUDIO FLYWHEEL STRATEGY V1

Status: **WORKING OPERATING MODEL**

## Core idea
We are not building isolated products. We are building connected intellectual-property worlds where each format strengthens the others.

The same character, story, mystery, lesson, visual asset, or software mechanic should be reused across as many compatible channels as possible.

## The flywheel

**BOOK / STORY**
creates characters, themes, mysteries, lessons, dialogue, worlds, and episode material.

↓

**CARTOON / ANIMATION**
makes the characters emotionally real, creates discoverable short-form media, and gives the audience a reason to care about the world.

↓

**GAME / INTERACTIVE SOFTWARE**
lets the audience do what the characters do: investigate, classify evidence, solve puzzles, ask better questions, discover hidden systems, and practice the lesson through play.

↓

**WEBSITE / DIGITAL HUB**
connects the books, cartoons, games, samples, character guides, mysteries, downloads, email audience, and future commerce.

↓

**AUDIENCE + REVENUE**
reveals what people actually care about and which property deserves more investment.

↓

**UPGRADE THE WINNERS**
use revenue and audience signals to improve animation quality, expand games, produce longer episodes, build richer software, publish more books, and eventually fund premium film/series production.

Then the stronger world feeds the loop again.

## Studio rule
**Do not wait for premium production quality before testing whether the story works.**

Story, character, lesson, humor, mystery, and usefulness must carry the early version.

Visual and technical quality may improve later.

## Reuse doctrine
Whenever we create an asset, ask:

1. Can this appear in the cartoon?
2. Can this become a game mechanic?
3. Can this guide people around the website?
4. Can this promote or support a book?
5. Can this become a short social clip?
6. Can this be reused in another episode?
7. Can this become part of a larger film/series later?

If yes, design it so reuse is easy.

## Portfolio rule
Every project should do at least one of three jobs:

1. **Earn money.**
2. **Build audience.**
3. **Prove or improve a reusable production system.**

The best projects do all three.

## What we should avoid
- Building expensive animation before proving audience interest.
- Creating one-off art that cannot be reused.
- Making games disconnected from the stories.
- Making cartoons that do not point back toward the larger world.
- Building website features that do not help discovery, engagement, or conversion.
- Treating books, games, cartoons, software, and film as separate companies.

## The company we are becoming
A story-first software and media studio where:

- stories create worlds,
- cartoons create attachment,
- games create participation,
- software creates utility and repeat engagement,
- the website connects everything,
- audience behavior tells us what to fund next,
- and successful properties finance deeper versions of themselves.
