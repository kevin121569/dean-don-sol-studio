# LIVING LEARNING OBJECTS — EPISODE 001
# “I’VE GOT YOU” — SCENE-BY-SCENE OUTLINE v1

Status: **DEVELOPMENT OUTLINE / NOT FINAL SCRIPT**

## Episode promise

Percy believes being parentheses mostly means standing around other people’s work and making it look pretty. When a set of related clue cards keeps being separated by a real physical process, the group can identify, measure, connect, revise, remember, and question — but nobody can keep the complete relationship intact long enough to finish the job.

Percy discovers that her paired shape can physically bracket an already-related set and hold that grouping together.

She does not become a shield. She does not pull anything toward herself. She does not decide what belongs together.

The moment works because the pieces already belong together, the group has already established that relationship, and the pieces are physically within the span of her two parenthesis halves.

Her first extraordinary line is earned in action:

**“I’ve got you.”**

## Story shape

This outline is designed as a compact animated pilot. Exact runtime is intentionally unlocked until script and storyboard passes.

The story should feel like an adventure first and an educational object-world second. No character announces the lesson. The viewer should understand Percy’s discovery visually before anybody explains it.

## Core physical problem

The group is preparing a four-card **Treasure Trail** for a young visitor arriving later. Each card contains one step in a sequence. Any single card is incomplete; order and relationship matter.

The cards are being assembled on a worktable beside a small paper-sorting / transfer mechanism used to move finished materials across the room.

A sudden air-pressure pulse from the transfer system lifts the loose cards and sends them onto different moving paths.

This gives the episode:
- a believable non-villain disruption,
- visible moving stakes,
- multiple opportunities for ordinary character functions,
- a final physical situation where the entire related set can be recovered onto one tray but still cannot stay grouped long enough to pass the sorter junction.

The machine is not malicious and does not become a monster. It is simply doing what it was built to do.

## SCENE 1 — THE BUSIEST TABLE IN THE ROOM

**Purpose:** introduce the ensemble through action, not roll call.

The Treasure Trail is almost ready.

Pencil drafts the title on the first card. Eraser removes a crooked word and Pencil redraws it without embarrassment. Ruler checks whether two card edges line up. Sticky Note holds one tiny reminder: **“Card 3 points LEFT.”** Hue highlights the actual direction word instead of coloring the whole card. Cursor helps the group choose between two layout options. Clipper fastens a reference sheet to the back of the packet.

Mobi sits visibly as a phone near the work, available but not narrating everything.

Percy stands around the finished title block in a visually elegant parenthesis pose.

Someone — warmly, without cruelty — says the arrangement looks beautiful.

Percy smiles because she likes being beautiful.

Then she notices everyone else immediately return to a job.

Her smile stays, but a tiny question lands.

**Visual rule:** no exposition explaining powers. Everybody is simply doing ordinary object work.

## SCENE 2 — GRANDPA’S BENT LITTLE JOB

**Purpose:** establish Percy/Clipper affection and seed the emotional question.

Clipper is slightly bent from age and stubbornly trying to fasten a stack that is one sheet too thick.

Percy teases him for doing things “the ancient way.”

Clipper replies that the ancient way still works when the pages want to wander.

Percy asks, almost joking:

> “What do parentheses do when the pages want to wander?”

Clipper does **not** explain her future ability.

He gives the simplest literal answer he knows:

> “You group things.”

Percy looks at herself around the already-neat title.

> “So… decoration with grammar.”

Clipper snorts and goes back to work.

The beat is funny, not sad yet.

## SCENE 3 — THE PUFF

**Purpose:** inciting disruption.

The paper-transfer mechanism engages for an unrelated scheduled cycle.

A pressure flap opens with a loud **WHUFF**.

The four loose Treasure Trail cards lift off the table.

One slaps against Hue.
One sails under Ruler.
Two land on the moving transfer surface.

Before anyone can grab them, diverter gates separate the moving cards onto different tracks.

No villain. No magical storm. A normal system plus loose paper equals trouble.

Percy instinctively opens her two halves wider — but the cards are already too far apart and outside her span.

Nothing happens.

That failure is important: she cannot remote-grab them.

## SCENE 4 — WHAT DO WE ACTUALLY KNOW?

**Purpose:** let Mobi help without solving.

Everyone talks at once about where the cards “must have gone.”

Mobi is asked what they know.

Mobi separates observation from guess:

- Card 1 hit Hue and fell near the worktable.
- Card 2 passed the blue diverter.
- Card 3 was last seen before the split.
- Card 4’s path was not observed.

Mobi says, in simple language:

> “We have three observations. We do not know where Card Four went.”

Then:

> “What can we check next?”

Mobi creates the next useful question rather than producing the missing card’s location.

## SCENE 5 — EVERYBODY HAS A MOVE

**Purpose:** action montage that demonstrates distinct ordinary functions.

Hue spots the tiny printed arrow on a card that would otherwise blend into the conveyor markings.

Ruler compares the gap widths at two transfer paths and rules out one route because the card physically could not have passed through it flat.

Sticky Note remembers: **“Card 3 points LEFT.”** That tells the group which recovered card is Card 3 even though its number is face-down.

Cursor makes the route choice explicit: check the left return tray first.

Pencil marks the search route on scrap paper.

Eraser removes one route after new evidence proves it wrong.

The sequence should be quick, funny, and visual. Nobody says “My function is measuring.” They just measure.

Percy follows, trying to find a useful place to stand.

## SCENE 6 — THE PRETTY JOB

**Purpose:** turn Percy’s private doubt into story pressure without melodrama.

The group recovers two cards and sets them temporarily on a small tray.

Percy tries to help by standing around them as she did around the title.

A moving guide arm approaches. Percy’s position would block the mechanism.

A friend gently asks her to step aside so the tray can pass.

Nobody is dismissing her worth; it is simply the wrong place and wrong moment for her function.

Percy steps away.

The two cards move safely through.

Everyone celebrates the progress.

Percy glances at the neat little parentheses printed in the corner of one clue and quietly straightens herself.

## SCENE 7 — CLIPPER CAN HOLD TWO

**Purpose:** clarify Clipper/Percy distinction through action.

Two recovered cards begin sliding apart on an angled staging board.

Clipper fastens them together.

Problem solved — for those two.

Then the group finds the third card.

Clipper cannot fasten all three in the correct reading arrangement without covering information needed on the fronts.

He tries a second angle. Bad fit.

He shrugs:

> “I can fasten pages. I can’t make every page into one page.”

This is not his failure as a character. It is simply the boundary of a paper clip.

Percy watches closely.

## SCENE 8 — FALSE FINISH

**Purpose:** midpoint success that collapses for a reason.

Card 4 is finally found in a return pocket after the group follows evidence rather than guesses.

All four Treasure Trail cards are recovered.

For one second, everyone cheers.

They arrange the cards correctly on a transfer tray.

The trail reads as a complete sequence for the first time.

Then the transfer system starts its next cycle.

The tray tilts toward a junction.

The four loose cards begin sliding in different directions again.

Clipper can grab only two without covering the route marks.

Hue can show which card matters next, but cannot stop it moving.

Ruler can tell exactly how far they are separating, which is hilariously unhelpful in the moment.

Mobi cannot move physical objects.

The problem is no longer **finding** the set.

The problem is **keeping an already-established set together**.

## SCENE 9 — “EVERYBODY DOES SOMETHING.”

**Purpose:** Percy emotional low point and Grandpa beat.

The system pauses briefly before the next transfer pulse.

Percy looks at everyone crowded around the tray.

Quietly:

> “Everybody does something.”

Clipper is still wrestling with his impossible two-card compromise.

Without looking up:

> “So do you.”

Percy waits for the explanation.

Clipper has none.

He finally looks at her:

> “You think I came out of the box knowing everything about being bent?”

That gets the smallest reluctant smile from Percy.

No motivational speech follows.

A warning click from the transfer system ends the conversation.

## SCENE 10 — THE RELATIONSHIP BECOMES THE PROBLEM

**Purpose:** set up Percy’s discovery using relationship language rather than location alone.

The tray begins moving again.

The four cards are now all physically present on the same tray and within a span Percy could bracket.

The group calls out what must stay together:

> “Card One starts it!”

> “Two leads to Three!”

> “Don’t separate the question from its answer!”

> “We need the whole trail!”

The relationship has already been established by the story and by the recovered sequence.

Percy looks at the four cards.

Then at her own two halves.

Then at Clipper holding two related sheets together with his entire little metal body.

Nobody tells Percy what to do.

## SCENE 11 — PERCY MOVES

**Purpose:** first extraordinary manifestation.

Percy’s two parenthesis halves move apart.

They do not glow.

They do not shoot energy.

They physically take position on opposite sides of the four-card set.

The cards are already on the tray. Percy does not pull them toward herself.

She closes inward just enough to bracket the complete sequence.

The tray tilts.

The cards slide — but now their edges meet Percy’s parenthesis halves instead of scattering into separate channels.

The visual reads instantly:

`( Card 1  Card 2  Card 3  Card 4 )`

Percy freezes in surprise.

So does everyone else.

The machine keeps moving.

## SCENE 12 — “I’VE GOT YOU.”

**Purpose:** emotional payoff earned by physical action.

At the edge of the set, Card 4 begins slipping under one parenthesis curve.

Percy adjusts that side inward.

No remote force. No bubble. Just the parenthesis physically maintaining the grouping.

Percy looks at the slipping card — and, by extension, at the whole group she thought she merely decorated.

She says it softly the first time:

> **“I’ve got you.”**

The set clears the junction intact.

Only after it is safe does everyone explode into celebration.

Do not bury the line under music or chatter. Give it room.

## SCENE 13 — NOW EVERYBODY CAN FINISH

**Purpose:** prevent Percy from becoming the sole solver.

Percy holds the grouped cards long enough for the team to finish the Treasure Trail.

Hue focuses attention on the decisive direction word.

Ruler confirms two route distances are actually different.

Pencil completes a missing mark.

Eraser removes the group’s earlier wrong assumption.

Sticky Note supplies the one instruction they deliberately preserved.

Cursor makes the final route choice visible.

Mobi summarizes only what is now supported:

> “The four cards form one complete route. We have the whole trail.”

Percy made the solution possible. The group completes it together.

## SCENE 14 — GRANDPA KNOWS THAT LOOK

**Purpose:** quiet Clipper/Percy payoff.

Percy releases the finished set carefully.

She looks at her own two parenthesis halves as though seeing them for the first time.

Clipper straightens as much as a bent old paper clip can.

He gives her a proud side-eye.

Optional line, only if performance makes the affection unmistakable:

> “Huh. Knew you were good for more than decoration.”

Percy gives him a royal glare.

Then she leans one parenthesis lightly against him.

Clipper pretends not to notice.

## SCENE 15 — THE SPACE AROUND THE THING

**Purpose:** end image communicates belonging without a speech.

The Treasure Trail packet is finished on the worktable.

Earlier, Percy stood beside everyone else’s work.

Now the team naturally arranges themselves so Percy is around the completed packet while the others occupy their useful places inside and beside the work.

Nobody tells her where to stand.

Nobody calls it a lesson.

It simply looks right.

Hue, overexcited by the celebration, starts highlighting almost the entire finished packet.

Everyone turns toward him.

Hue slowly lowers the marker.

> “Too much important?”

Cut on the laugh.

## Percy emotional progression

1. **Comfortable:** Percy likes being elegant and pretty.
2. **Question:** everyone else seems to have an obvious practical job.
3. **Misread:** a harmless compliment makes her wonder whether beauty is all she contributes.
4. **Wrong fit:** she tries to help where her function is not useful and must step aside.
5. **Observation:** she watches Clipper’s humble fastening function matter in a real crisis.
6. **Low point:** “Everybody does something.”
7. **Need appears:** the group does not need another finder, measurer, or answer-giver; it needs a relationship physically kept intact.
8. **Discovery:** Percy brackets what already belongs together.
9. **Identity shift:** parentheses are not merely something around the work; their placement can preserve meaning.
10. **Belonging:** the final image places Percy around the shared work because that is where she naturally fits.

Beauty is never rejected or punished. Percy gets to be pretty **and** useful.

## Mobi lock for Episode 001

Mobi may:
- report observations already available to the group,
- distinguish observation from guess,
- identify what remains unknown,
- ask what evidence can be checked next,
- summarize the final supported route.

Mobi may not:
- know where Card 4 went without evidence,
- identify off-screen events,
- tell Percy what her deeper ability is,
- solve the physical grouping problem,
- replace another character’s function,
- become an unrestricted child-facing live chat mechanic through this episode.

For any interactive adaptation aimed at the core 7–11 audience, Mobi’s prompts remain authored, curated, menu-based, or otherwise constrained unless a separate child-facing AI review explicitly approves something broader.

## Percy physical-rule lock for Episode 001

Every extraordinary Percy beat must satisfy all six checks:

1. The relationship among the enclosed things is already supported by the story.
2. The related things are physically together before Percy acts.
3. The full set is within the span of her two parenthesis halves.
4. Both parenthesis halves visibly bracket the set.
5. Percy prevents separation by physical containment; she does not attract, tether, or remotely move objects.
6. Removing the visible parentheses from the shot would make the action stop making sense.

If any storyboard violates one of these, revise the storyboard rather than expanding Percy’s power.

## Education-without-worksheet lock

The episode may model:
- observation vs. inference,
- useful measurement,
- revision after evidence,
- remembering one deliberate fact,
- explicit choice,
- grouping related information.

It may **not** stop for a character to define these terms to the audience.

The adventure carries the idea.

## Optional transmedia hooks after the episode

These are **post-story** activities and must not interrupt the pilot.

### Percy — Group what belongs together
Players receive already-seen cards and group them into supported sets. The mechanic never silently reveals a relationship the player has not earned.

### Mobi — What do we know?
A constrained prompt asks the player to classify three statements as **observed**, **guess**, or **still unknown** using only evidence from the episode.

### Sticky Note — Keep one thing
The player chooses one known instruction to carry into a short bonus puzzle.

### Hue — What matters here?
The player highlights the exact words that answer a question. Highlighting everything produces a humorous “Too much important” response rather than punishment.

## Outline red-team checklist

Before script pages are authorized, confirm:

- Percy never remote-grabs a card.
- Percy never creates the relationship she protects.
- Percy’s first extraordinary action remains visible bracketing.
- “I’ve got you” occurs only after the physical action begins.
- Clipper does not explain Percy’s ability in advance.
- Clipper remains competent and useful.
- No supporting character is made foolish merely so Percy can shine.
- Mobi preserves uncertainty and does not reveal unseen information.
- The recovered cards are physically brought together before Percy contains them.
- The machine remains a believable physical process, not a villain substitute.
- The lesson remains embedded in action.
- The ending returns to humor after the emotional payoff.
