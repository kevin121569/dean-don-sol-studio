# LIVING LEARNING OBJECTS — PUNCTUATION CHARACTER BENCH v1

Status: **OPTIONAL FUTURE CHARACTER BENCH / NOT YET CORE CAST**
Tracked under Issue #23 / PR #24.

## Purpose

Keep promising punctuation-family characters available without forcing them into Episode 001 or bloating the core cast. New characters enter only when a story problem genuinely needs their real punctuation function.

## Brac the Bracket `[ ]`

**Relationship:** Percy's brother.

**Working concept:** Brac is direct, practical, and boundary-minded. Where Percy groups related material with parentheses, Brac can help mark inserted clarification, extra context, or a clearly bounded set when that function naturally fits the lesson.

### Design rule

- instantly recognizable as square brackets,
- same low-cost illustrated language as Percy,
- simple body/clothes if personality benefits,
- no power unrelated to the real bracket function,
- no generic forcefield or cage behavior,
- can be funny about boundaries, rules, and 'putting something in its proper place' without teaching an inaccurate definition.

## Curly / the Curly Braces `{ }`

**Relationship:** intentionally flexible for now — cousin, parent, family friend, or recurring ally depending on the story.

**Working concept:** Curly is an organizer. Curly braces can visually group a collection or set, making them useful for stories about organizing many related things under one idea.

### Design rule

- unmistakably curly braces,
- one coordinated character unless a later story earns another choice,
- simple illustrated body language,
- grouping/organization must stay connected to the real symbol,
- do not turn Curly into generic magic hands or a containment field.

## Family / relationship doctrine

Only Brac's relationship is presently fixed: **Brac is Percy's brother.**

The rest of the punctuation family tree should remain loose until a story benefits from making it specific. We do not need to explain an entire punctuation genealogy to the audience.

Possible future relationships may include:

- Curly as cousin,
- older brace characters as parents or mentors,
- punctuation friends from school/work,
- visiting relatives whose function drives one specific story.

## Story-entry rule

A punctuation character is introduced only if all three are true:

1. the episode has a problem their real punctuation function can help solve,
2. their personality adds something the existing cast cannot,
3. the lesson still works as a story rather than becoming a grammar lecture.

If the character is only there because the symbol exists, keep them on the bench.

## Tone target

The punctuation family can create recurring jokes and warmth, but the series is not a punctuation sitcom. Their relationships serve story, emotion, and learning.

Example running joke territory:

- Percy thinks Brac is too square.
- Brac thinks Percy always has an aside.
- Curly has a habit of organizing everybody into groups.

The joke may point toward function, but the character must remain more than the joke.
