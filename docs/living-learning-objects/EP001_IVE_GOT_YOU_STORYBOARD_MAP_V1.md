## Visual thesis

The episode should be understandable as a silent short before it is understandable as a lesson.

The camera must make three ideas visible without exposition:

1. **Everybody has a recognizable ordinary function.**
2. **Percy cannot affect distant objects or invent relationships.**
3. **When her moment arrives, the extraordinary action is simply parentheses visibly doing parentheses work.**

The Percy reveal succeeds only if a child watching with the sound off can point at the screen and understand:

`( these belong together )`

No glow, aura, forcefield, telekinetic pull, magical tether, speed-line burst, superhero pose, or energy effect may substitute for readable physical bracketing.

---

## Global visual grammar

### Camera

- Favor clear medium and wide compositions over frantic cutting.
- Close-ups are reserved for evidence, character reaction, and the Percy discovery.
- Do not use heroic low-angle framing on Percy before or during the discovery; the power of the moment comes from recognition, not superhero language.
- Machine geography must be established before the cards scatter so later movement reads as cause-and-effect rather than arbitrary danger.
- Reaction shots should be short and character-specific; avoid a sequence where every cast member receives an identical reaction close-up.

### Motion

- Ordinary object mechanics should drive character movement: Clipper flexes, Hue marks, Cursor points/clicks, Ruler aligns, Sticky adheres/hops, Eraser rubs, Pencil draws.
- Percy’s two halves move as one character with two coordinated bodies. They can separate spatially, but their acting intention remains unified.
- Percy’s first failed reach must be unmistakable: she opens toward distant cards and nothing crosses the gap.
- Percy’s successful containment must be contact-based: the moving cards physically meet her curves.

### Color

- Keep the Learning Lab warm and inviting, with neutral machinery and paper surfaces so character colors read cleanly.
- Hue’s yellow is functional emphasis, not general scene lighting.
- Percy should not acquire a new color during discovery.
- No discovery glow.

### Sound-off test

Every major story turn must remain legible with dialogue muted:

- loose cards + active transfer system = problem,
- Mobi does not magically locate missing cards,
- functions narrow the search,
- Percy is the wrong tool once,
- Clipper can physically hold some but not all,
- complete four-card relationship is visible,
- Percy brackets it,
- Card Four slips,
- Percy adjusts and saves the grouping,
- team finishes together.

---

## Environment continuity — machine geography

Use one master geography and preserve it through every shot:

**LEFT:** main worktable and character staging area.  
**CENTER:** safe accessible transfer trays / return pockets.  
**RIGHT:** guarded moving transfer path and final outbound split.  
**BACKGROUND:** visible status panel with green/amber cycle light and two outbound-bin arrows near final junction.

The characters may work only on the worktable, outer walkway, safe trays, and return pockets. Moving internal machinery remains behind a clear physical boundary.

The audience should see the final junction at least once before Scene 8 so the later threat is spatially familiar.

# Percy reveal continuity lock

The storyboard and later animation must preserve this visual chain exactly:

1. **Scene 3:** Percy attempts a distant reach; nothing moves.
2. **Scene 6:** she can stand around cards, but mere position is not automatically useful.
3. **Scene 7:** she observes Clipper’s direct-contact fastening.
4. **Scene 10:** the four-card relationship becomes visibly established.
5. **Scene 11:** all four cards are already physically together and within her span.
6. Both parenthesis halves move into visible positions on opposite sides.
7. Cards begin sliding only after Percy is in place.
8. Card edges physically contact Percy’s curves.
9. Contact prevents the set from separating.
10. Card 4 slips; Percy adjusts by moving her right half inward.
11. Only then: **“I’ve got you.”**

Any storyboard frame, animation prompt, generated clip, or edit that implies attraction, telekinesis, invisible force, remote grabbing, teleportation, or a relationship created by Percy is rejected.

# Character silhouette / continuity notes

## Percy
- One character, always represented by two coordinated parenthesis halves.
- Both halves should normally remain visible in the same shot when her function is story-relevant.
- Do not let AI generation collapse her into one bracket, duplicate her as twins, turn her into braces/brackets, or add limbs/effects that obscure the parenthesis silhouette.

## Clipper
- Preserve the permanent slight bend.
- His bend is age/personality, not injury shorthand.
- When he fastens paper, the paper must visibly pass through/under his clip geometry.

## Hue
- Always reads first as a real highlighter.
- Yellow mark appears only where he physically marks.
- No autonomous glowing trail.

## Mobi
- Always a mobile phone.
- Screen content must derive from shared story information.
- Do not add floating holograms, omniscient maps, satellite feeds, or machine-control overlays.

## Cursor
- Reads as selection/pointing, not magic telekinesis.

## Sticky Note
- Written reminder remains short and stable; do not morph text between shots.

## Ruler
- Measurement marks / scale stay consistent enough that comparisons do not visually contradict dialogue.

# AI-animation production notes

These are production safeguards, not story changes.

- Build and reuse locked reference images for every character before generating moving shots.
- For Percy reveal shots 11C–12D, prefer image-to-video or controlled keyframe workflows over pure text-to-video so her paired shape cannot drift.
- Generate the four Treasure Trail cards as fixed reusable assets with exact text and numbering; do not rely on video generation to redraw readable text every frame.
- Composite card text in post when needed rather than accepting hallucinated lettering.
- Treat transfer-system geometry as a fixed environment plate or reference so outbound split, trays, and safety boundary do not migrate between shots.
- Keep shot 11D and shot 12D simple enough that silhouette clarity survives imperfect model motion.
- Generate no discovery VFX layer because the absence of VFX is canon.
- Use separate passes/layers for Hue’s highlight marks when model consistency is weak.
- Verify Percy’s two halves do not swap orientation during cuts or generated motion.

# Storyboard red-team checklist

Before visual lock, confirm:

- A child can explain Percy’s successful action with the sound off.
- A child can also see why Percy’s Scene 3 reach failed.
- Scene 6 does not accidentally imply Percy already used the power successfully.
- The four-card relationship is visually established before Percy acts.
- Both Percy halves are visible in the hero bracket shot.
- Cards physically contact Percy in the success sequence.
- No VFX implies an invisible field.
- Mobi never displays unseen or privileged information.
- Machine geography does not jump between scenes.
- Nobody enters moving machinery.
- Clipper’s competence is clear before his limitation.
- Supporting functions feel like character behavior, not educational demonstrations.
- Percy’s “I’ve got you” is not interrupted by a cutaway or visual spectacle.

- Grandpa teasing reads as affectionate family rhythm.
- Final composition communicates belonging without dialogue.
