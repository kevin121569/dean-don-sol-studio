# PERCY 30–45 SECOND PRODUCTION BUILD SHEET v1

Status: **PRODUCTION TEST — PERCY APPROVED / CLIPPER APPROVED**
Tracked by GitHub Issue #23 / PR #24.

## Purpose

Build one finished, inexpensive 30–45 second cartoon proof that demonstrates the actual Living Learning Objects production method:

- strong characters,
- simple drawings,
- limited motion,
- voice-first storytelling,
- readable educational idea,
- reusable art for the website.

This is not a concept reel. It is the first production test.

## Locked character direction

### Percy

Percy is an ordinary, friendly illustrated girl in her everyday state. Her special working function is revealed when her head/face opens into two parenthesis forms that can move to either side of an already-related set and keep it together.

Rules:

- everyday Percy must read as a normal character first,
- working Percy must still unmistakably be the same Percy,
- transformation is simple and graphic, not a VFX spectacle,
- no body morphing beyond the head/face opening into the two parentheses,
- no forcefield,
- no telekinesis,
- no stretching/growing to fit the problem,
- one personality and one voice before, during, and after the split.

### Clipper

Clipper is visually locked in the approved Grandpa paper-clip direction:

- recognizable paper clip silhouette,
- warm older face,
- glasses / mustache / simple clothes allowed,
- calm, encouraging, slightly amused delivery,
- no redesign for this test.

## Target runtime

**Approx. 41 seconds.**

The test may land anywhere from 35–45 seconds after voice timing and edit rhythm are finalized.

## Shot build

### SHOT 1 — NORMAL PERCY / SETUP — 4 sec

**Visual:** Wide/medium shot. Percy and Clipper at the Sorting Facility. Mostly held art. Tiny eye blink or head turn only.

**Percy:** “A new day, a new puzzle.”

**Clipper:** small pleased chuckle or smile; no line required.

**Motion budget:** camera push or gentle parallax only.

**Sound:** light room tone, conveyor hum, tiny musical motif.

---

### SHOT 2 — THE PROBLEM — 4 sec

**Visual:** Four Treasure Trail cards already related and near one another begin sliding apart on the safe exposed surface.

**Percy:** “Oh no. They’re moving away!”

**Motion budget:** cards slide. Percy can remain offscreen.

**Sound:** soft mechanical movement; no danger alarm.

---

### SHOT 3 — SHE TRIES — 5 sec

**Visual:** Percy reaches toward the cards with ordinary hands but cannot keep the whole set together. Do not imply remote pulling.

**Percy:** “I can’t keep them together…”

**Motion budget:** one reach pose + one worried expression swap.

**Sound:** music briefly thins.

---

### SHOT 4 — REALIZATION — 5 sec

**Visual:** Close-up Percy. Hold mostly still. Blink, thinking look, then small smile.

**Percy:** “Wait…”

Beat.

**Percy:** “That’s what I’m for.”

**Motion budget:** eye shift, blink, mouth swap, tiny head lift.

**Sound:** one small discovery note, not magical sparkle.

---

### SHOT 5 — PERCY OPENS — 6 sec

**Visual:** Percy’s familiar head/face separates graphically into the two parenthesis halves. The rest of the transformation is deliberately simple.

**Percy:** “Okay. Stay together.”

**Motion budget:** one clean separation move. No morphing spectacle, glow, particles, or squash-and-stretch.

**Sound:** simple paper/ink/wooden slide-like design cue rather than superhero effect.

---

### SHOT 6 — BRACKET MOVE — 6 sec

**Visual:** The two normal-size Percy halves move to opposite sides of the four cards. The cards are already visibly one related group.

**Percy:** softly, concentrating: “Together…”

**Motion budget:** left half translates left; right half translates right/inward. Cards make physical contact with her curves when they try to separate.

**Sound:** gentle contact ticks from cards; music builds emotionally.

---

### SHOT 7 — “I’VE GOT YOU.” — 6 sec

**Visual:** One outer card slips. Percy’s right half shifts inward just enough to catch it. Hold after success.

**Percy:** “I’ve got you.”

**Performance:** quiet, warm, certain — not superhero triumph.

**Motion budget:** one inward adjustment, blink/eye soften, then hold.

**Sound:** musical resolution only after the card is secure.

---

### SHOT 8 — CLIPPER’S PROUD BEAT — 5 sec

**Visual:** Percy back in normal form beside Clipper. Mostly held drawing.

**Clipper:** “That’s it, Percy. You bring things together.”

**Percy:** small smile.

Optional final button if timing allows:

**Percy:** “I’m still pretty, though.”

**Clipper:** “Obviously.”

**Motion budget:** mouth swaps, blink, tiny shoulder/head movement.

**Sound:** warm musical button.

## Motion ceiling

For this production test, the preferred motion stack is:

1. held illustration,
2. eye/blink,
3. mouth swap,
4. tiny hand/head gesture,
5. camera push/pan,
6. card slide,
7. Percy split/translate,
8. one small recovery motion.

No scene should require full-body frame-by-frame animation.

## Voice direction

### Percy

Young, bright, curious, emotionally sincere. Avoid exaggerated preschool voice. She should sound like a real kid with a strong personality rather than a mascot.

Her “I’ve got you” line is the emotional test of the character: soft, protective, and confident.

### Clipper

Warm older male voice with humor and patience. He should sound like someone who has seen a lot, knows more than he says, and enjoys watching Percy discover things herself.

## Website reuse package

The cartoon production assets should be exported so the site can reuse them without extra character redesign:

- Percy normal idle,
- Percy thinking,
- Percy pointing/helping,
- Percy split/working icon,
- Clipper idle,
- Clipper speaking/helpful,
- transparent-background PNG/WebP exports,
- one lightweight idle loop per character only if it remains cheap.

Possible website jobs:

- guide visitors through story/game areas,
- point at controls or clues,
- deliver one-line hints,
- introduce free sample activities,
- react to completed puzzles,
- host kid-safe authored interactions.

Website Percy/Clipper must remain the same characters as the cartoon, not separate mascot redesigns.

## Production pass criteria

The test passes if:

1. Percy is lovable before the special ability appears.
2. A child can understand what went wrong without explanation.
3. Percy’s split reads as a simple natural function of her design.
4. The cards are kept together through visible physical bracketing, not magic.
5. Clipper feels warm and memorable with almost no animation.
6. The scene is emotionally clear with minimal motion.
7. The style can be reproduced for many episodes without expensive character animation.
8. The same Percy and Clipper assets can be reused on the website.

## Production philosophy proved by this test

**The story, voice, character, and idea carry the episode. Animation only moves what needs to move.**

If this 30–45 second scene works, the studio has a repeatable low-cost cartoon language for Living Learning Objects and a production method that can later be adapted to other properties, including darker illustrated projects such as The Harness.
