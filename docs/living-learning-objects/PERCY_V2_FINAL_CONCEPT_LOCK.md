# LIVING LEARNING OBJECTS — PERCY v2 FINAL CONCEPT LOCK

Status: **APPROVED CHARACTER-DIRECTION / SIMPLE PRODUCTION LOCK**  
Parent: `REFERENCE_STYLE_LOCK_V1.md`  
Tracked by GitHub Issue #23 / PR #24.

## Core production goal

Percy must be simple enough that we can actually make episodes.

The show is not trying to win an animation-budget contest. It is trying to tell memorable stories, teach children in a fun way, and make characters kids care about.

If Percy can be understood, performed, and emotionally felt with a mostly still drawing, a voice, a blink, a mouth change, and one clear action, the design is working.

## Percy in one sentence

Percy is a friendly, slightly vain, thoughtful girl-like character whose ordinary head appears normal and complete, but whose face/head can separate into two parenthesis halves when she needs to hold an already-related group together.

## Everyday Percy

In normal scenes Percy should read first as a person, not as a visual effect.

Use:

- a simple round/oval face area visually completed by the two parenthesis side-shapes,
- one complete personality and one complete voice,
- minimal stick/wire body,
- simple arms and legs,
- ordinary shoes,
- optional simple clothing such as a plain shirt, skirt, scarf, bow, or small hair accent,
- expressive eyes and eyebrows,
- very simple mouth swaps.

The body is not the character. The body only lets Percy stand, walk, gesture, and wear a little personality.

Do not add complex anatomy, detailed clothing folds, elaborate hair simulation, or anything that makes every shot expensive to reproduce.

## Head / face concept

Percy should look as though her head is one complete face in everyday life.

The parenthesis forms should be integrated into the outside edges of that face so the audience does not initially experience them as two separate creatures.

The key visual trick is:

**Together: one face.  
Apart: two parenthesis halves of the same face.**

When separated:

- the left half retains the left-eye / left-brow / left-cheek side of her expression,
- the right half retains the right-eye / right-brow / right-cheek side,
- she is still one person,
- one voice continues across both halves,
- neither side gets its own personality,
- neither side becomes a twin or separate character.

The effect can feel surprising the first time without requiring a complicated transformation.

## Working mode

Percy separates only when the story gives her a real reason.

For Episode 001:

1. The related objects are already identified.
2. They are already physically together.
3. Percy realizes they need to remain together.
4. Her head/face opens into the two parenthesis halves.
5. The halves move to opposite sides of the group.
6. They physically bracket the group.
7. She keeps the set from separating.
8. She says: **“I’ve got you.”**

No glow. No forcefield. No telekinesis. No resizing. No superhero transformation.

The reveal should be understandable from silhouette and movement alone.

## Transformation budget

The transformation is intentionally cheap.

Preferred method:

- normal Percy drawing,
- one held reaction drawing,
- one split pose,
- two parenthesis pieces translate apart,
- optional small eye/brow change,
- voice sells the emotional realization.

No fluid morph animation is required.

A cut, cross-dissolve, or two- to four-pose sequence is acceptable if it reads clearly.

## Motion budget

Default Percy performance should use, in order:

1. voice over held drawing,
2. blink,
3. eye shift,
4. mouth swap,
5. eyebrow change,
6. tiny hand gesture,
7. head/body tilt,
8. short walk/slide,
9. rare split-apart action.

The split is special partly because Percy does not constantly move in elaborate ways before it happens.

## Clothes and personality

Clothes are allowed and encouraged when they make Percy feel like Percy.

Good examples:

- simple sneakers,
- a small bow or hair tie,
- a plain top,
- a simple skirt or shorts,
- one recurring accent item.

Avoid:

- outfits that change every shot,
- detailed fabric simulation,
- accessories that obscure the parenthesis silhouette,
- clothing that requires complex redraws for every pose.

A child should still recognize Percy if the clothes are removed.

## Personality lock

Percy is:

- kind,
- pretty and aware that she is pretty,
- a little dramatic,
- funny without trying too hard,
- initially unsure whether she is useful,
- emotionally sincere when it matters,
- proud without becoming arrogant,
- affectionate with Clipper.

Her story does **not** teach that being pretty is bad or shallow.

Her discovery is that she can be pretty **and** useful.

## Voice rule

The voice does most of the acting.

Percy does not need constant body motion to communicate emotion.

A great Percy performance may consist of:

- one still drawing,
- one eyebrow change,
- one blink,
- one line delivered well.

That is acceptable finished production language.

## Child-understanding test

A child should be able to answer:

- Who is Percy? — The girl whose head/face can become parentheses.
- What can she do? — Hold things that already belong together.
- Is she two people? — No, she is one person with two halves.
- Does she use magic? — No, her shape physically brackets things.
- Why is it special? — She thought she was only decorative, then discovered what her shape could do.

If the design makes those answers harder, simplify it.

## Clipper relationship

Clipper remains the approved Grandpa design direction and should not be redesigned to match Percy.

Their visual contrast is useful:

- Percy: simple girl-like face/head with parenthesis function,
- Clipper: literal old paper clip with Grandpa personality.

The common language is not matching anatomy. The common language is simple ink drawing, limited color, personality, and easy production.

## Production rejection rules

Reject any Percy design or shot that:

- turns her into a glossy 3D mascot,
- makes her head a generic cartoon ball with parentheses pasted on afterward,
- creates two independent Percy characters when she separates,
- requires elaborate full-body animation to read the scene,
- hides the parenthesis identity under hair/clothes/effects,
- grows or stretches the parentheses,
- uses magic VFX to hold objects together,
- makes the body more visually important than the face/function,
- cannot be redrawn consistently for many episodes.

## Final direction

**Everyday Percy should look like a real little character who could walk into a simple illustrated story. Her special reveal is that the face/head we already know can open into the parentheses she was always made from.**

That is the hook.

That is enough.

Build the story and voice around it instead of increasing the graphics budget.
