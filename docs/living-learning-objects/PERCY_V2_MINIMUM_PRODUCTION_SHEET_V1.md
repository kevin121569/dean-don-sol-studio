# PERCY v2 — MINIMUM PRODUCTION SHEET v1

Status: **PRODUCTION STARTER LOCK**  
Tracked by GitHub Issue #23 / PR #24.

## Purpose

This is the minimum Percy asset set required to start making Episode 001 cheaply. Do not expand the design inventory unless a real scene requires it.

The production goal is not fancy animation. The goal is to make finished cartoons that can move, talk, tell stories, and teach kids in a fun way.

## Core design

Percy is a simple illustrated girl-like character whose identity is built around a face/head that can open into two parenthesis halves.

Normal Percy should look friendly, readable, and emotionally real enough that a child accepts her as a person before knowing what her deeper function is.

The body is secondary:
- simple stick/wire limbs,
- very simple torso/dress or clothing shape,
- optional shoes,
- no complicated anatomy,
- no expensive deformation requirements.

Her head/face is the important part.

## Asset 01 — Normal front pose

One clean full-body front pose.

Requirements:
- joined head/face reads as one character,
- underlying parenthesis construction is not obvious enough to spoil the discovery,
- simple clothing permitted,
- neutral friendly expression,
- black/ink line base,
- restrained spot color only,
- silhouette reproducible by hand or simple rig.

This becomes the default reusable standing Percy.

## Assets 02–06 — Five expression swaps

Use the same normal head/body construction. Do not redraw Percy as a new character for each expression.

Required expressions:
1. amused / smug,
2. curious,
3. uncertain / hurt without melodrama,
4. surprised by herself,
5. warm / relieved smile.

Expressions should be achievable mostly through:
- eyebrow changes,
- eye direction/shape,
- mouth swap,
- tiny head tilt.

No full-body acting is required to sell these emotions.

## Asset 07 — Working / split pose

Percy’s joined head opens into the two parenthesis halves.

Requirements:
- same character, same voice, same dimensions,
- no duplicate personality,
- no growth/stretching/scaling,
- no glow or forcefield,
- each half preserves enough facial information that the audience understands this is still Percy,
- body treatment may remain centered/minimal or simplify away during the functional shot if staging is clearer,
- the two halves move apart only as far as physically needed.

This is a compositional action, not an effects-heavy transformation.

## Asset 08 — “I’ve got you” hero frame

One production-quality still frame showing:

with Percy’s two normal-size head/parenthesis halves physically bracketing the already-related cards.

Requirements:

This frame is the primary visual proof for Episode 001.

## Motion budget

Default Percy animation uses only:
- blink,
- eye shift,
- mouth swap,
- tiny head tilt,
- small arm gesture,
- short body translation,
- camera pan/push,
- split/rejoin of the head halves when functionally required.

No walk cycle is required unless a scene truly needs one. No full lip-sync rig is required. Mouth swaps synced loosely to dialogue are acceptable.

## Voice-first rule

Percy’s personality is carried primarily by:
- writing,
- voice performance,
- pause/timing,
- expression swaps,
- composition.

A mostly still Percy delivering a strong line is valid finished animation.

## Clothing rule

Clothing may change by episode or story need, but Percy’s face/head construction stays consistent.

Clothes are personality shorthand, not a rigging burden. Prefer one simple outfit for Episode 001.

## Episode 001 minimum package

If we have these eight assets, we have enough Percy to start production:

- 1 normal front pose,
- 5 expression swaps,
- 1 working/split pose,
- 1 “I’ve got you” hero frame.

Do not delay Episode 001 waiting for a larger Percy model sheet.

## Acceptance test

PASS if:
- Percy reads as one likable character,
- the normal state does not spoil the parenthesis reveal,
- the split state is immediately understandable,
- the same artwork can be reused repeatedly,
- a short scene can work with voice + minimal motion,
- production remains cheap enough to make many episodes.

FAIL if:
- Percy requires full 3D rigging,
- the design depends on complex body animation,
- each shot requires redrawing from scratch,
- the split looks like two separate people,
- the reveal requires visual effects to understand.

## Production priority

Finish this minimum set, then test a real 20–40 second Episode 001 scene before designing more assets.
