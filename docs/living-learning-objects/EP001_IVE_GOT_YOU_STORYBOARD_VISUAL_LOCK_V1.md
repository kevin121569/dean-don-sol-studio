## LOCK 1 — PERCY NEVER RIDES INTO MOVING MACHINERY

The final grouping sequence occurs on a **guarded, exposed carrier surface** that remains visibly accessible from the characters’ safe side of the transfer system.

The audience must always be able to see that:

- Percy is on the exposed carrier/tray surface, not inside the machine,
- the guarded internal rollers/gears/diverter hardware remain physically separated behind the safety boundary,
- Percy never crosses that boundary,
- no character reaches into, climbs into, blocks, or rides inside moving machinery,
- the carrier surface transports the grouped packet past the visible outbound split while Percy remains on the safe, exposed surface.

### Required shot correction

For storyboard shots 11A–12F, stage the carrier as a shallow exposed platform traveling along the **outside face** of the guarded mechanism.

The final diverter split must be visible **beneath/beside the carrier path**, behind guarding, so the danger is separation into different bins rather than contact with machinery.

Do not frame Percy disappearing through a tunnel, doorway, enclosed chute, or machine housing.

If a generated shot makes it look as though Percy enters the mechanism, reject the shot even if the story action otherwise works.

## LOCK 2 — PERCY'S SHAPE DOES NOT RESIZE

Percy’s extraordinary discovery is **separation and coordinated placement of her existing two parenthesis halves**, not growth, stretching, scaling, transformation, or morphing.

Her parenthesis halves keep their established physical dimensions throughout Episode 001.

She may:

- move the two halves farther apart,
- move them closer together,
- translate or rotate slightly for readable physical contact,
- coordinate both halves as one character.

She may **not**:

- grow taller or wider,
- stretch to span a set that does not fit,
- scale up for a hero shot,
- become giant parentheses,
- change from parentheses into brackets/braces,
- extend invisible reach beyond her physical silhouette.

### Required staging consequence

Before Percy acts, the four Treasure Trail cards must already be arranged tightly enough that the complete set fits **within Percy’s normal established span** when her two halves separate to their plausible maximum distance.

The production must solve fit through:

- card size,
- card spacing,
- tray composition,
- camera angle,
- character blocking,

—not by changing Percy’s size.

If the four-card set cannot fit between her normal halves in a storyboard frame, revise the layout. Do not revise Percy.

## LOCKED HERO SEQUENCE — REVISED VISUAL CHAIN

The following chain is mandatory for shots 11A through 12F:

1. Wide shot establishes the four cards already together on the exposed carrier, final split visible, internal machinery guarded.
2. Percy steps onto/alongside the exposed carrier during the normal safe pause without crossing the safety boundary.
3. Her **normal-size** left half moves to the outside edge of Card 1.
4. Her **normal-size** right half moves to the outside edge of Card 4.
5. The full four-card set visibly fits between those unchanged halves.
6. Hold the clean hero image: `( 1   2   3   4 )`.
7. The carrier resumes along the exposed path.
8. The cards begin sliding outward from ordinary tray motion.
9. Their outside edges physically meet Percy’s curves.
10. Direct contact prevents separation.
11. Card 4 begins slipping under the right curve.
12. Percy moves the right half inward—without changing its size.
13. The card catches against the physical curve.
14. Percy says, softly: **“I’ve got you.”**
15. The exposed carrier passes the guarded outbound split with the set intact.
16. The receiving surface remains visibly accessible; Percy never disappears into machinery.
17. Only after the packet arrives whole does celebration begin.

## SOUND-OFF ACCEPTANCE TEST

A child watching without dialogue should be able to answer all five questions correctly:

1. **Why couldn’t Percy save the cards earlier?**  
   They were too far away from her physical reach.

2. **What changed later?**  
   The related cards were already together and close enough to fit between her two halves.

3. **What did Percy actually do?**  
   She put one parenthesis on each side and physically kept the cards from sliding apart.

4. **Did Percy grow or use invisible magic?**  
   No. She stayed the same size and moved her two halves.

5. **Did Percy go inside the machine?**  
   No. She stayed on the exposed safe carrier while the guarded system moved the packet past the split.

If any answer is visually ambiguous, the storyboard has not passed.

## AI-GENERATION REJECTION RULES

Reject any generated frame or clip that introduces:

- Percy scaling up or down during the reveal,
- rubber/stretch deformation of her curves,
- a third parenthesis half,
- one half disappearing,
- twins instead of one paired character,
- brackets/braces replacing parentheses,
- forcefield glow between the halves,
- cards moving toward Percy before physical contact,
- Percy entering a chute or enclosed machine interior,
- Percy clipping through guards/rollers,
- the final split moving to a different location between shots,
- a camera angle that hides whether contact is actually occurring.

A visually prettier generation does not outrank canon clarity.

---

## PRODUCTION-SAFE CAMERA RULES FOR THE REVEAL

- Prefer top-down / high three-quarter shots for the bracket-lock image so both curves and all four cards stay visible.
- Use a side/oblique insert only to prove card-edge contact against Percy’s curves.
- Do not use extreme perspective that makes one half appear larger than the other.
- Do not use a dolly/zoom effect that can be mistaken for Percy growing.
- Keep the safety boundary or guarded mechanism readable in at least one wide before and one wide after the close contact inserts.
- Do not cut from safe tray to a visually unrelated tunnel/interior angle during the crossing.
